Investigation of multi-trait EBMF program correspondence

This vignette extends the single-trait investigation (investigation-univariate-analysis.html) to multiple traits, and asks one question: which programs are shared between traits, which SNPs carry them, and do those SNPs push the traits the same way or opposite ways?

The default set runs this on real data with a strong, independently published prior to check against: the T2D GWAS upload from Suzuki T2D (GUID c6d2e20f-6ece-d093-78ee-37534547c466), whose high/medium programs turned out unusually clean, alongside three more specific metabolic traits rather than BMI – whose own background is dominated by dozens of body-size traits that move with it almost everywhere (see Investigation of univariate analysis) and so needs more care than a first cross-trait check calls for. Every cross-trait pair of programs is scored, from however many traits are supplied: triglyceride levels (1631), HDL cholesterol (1630) and HbA1c (1640), each paired with T2D and with each other. Suzuki et al.’s own cluster profiles give concrete expectations to check the links against (Suzuki T2D, Part 5):

  • Triglycerides should link to T2D’s Lipodystrophy and Metabolic syndrome programs (P2, P6, P10, P24, P31, P35, P47 in the T2D-only fit) and be mostly concordant, but with a possible antagonistic TM6SF2-type liver locus – the one place a single partner trait is expected to show both directions at once.
  • HDL cholesterol should link to the same Lipodystrophy/Metabolic syndrome programs, antagonistically (the T2D risk allele lowers HDL).
  • HbA1c should link to T2D’s beta-cell and Residual glycaemic signal instead – a different part of the partition from the lipid traits, and the only one of the three expected to touch T2D’s glycaemic core directly.

EBMF programs are discovered independently for each target trait, so each trait’s architecture is described in its own terms first. Every cross-trait pair of programs is then asked two questions. Same biology? – are the two programs driven by the same background traits in the same proportions (profile congruence of their trait loadings, L_pm)? Same SNPs? – where the two traits carry the same fine-mapped loci, do the programs load on them alike (alignment of their SNP loadings, F_pm)? The answers are combined into high / medium / low tiers; high- and medium-tier pairs are links, and links are grouped into multi-trait families, each with the set of loci its programs claim. The direction of a link – whether the alleles involved raise both traits or raise one and lower the other – comes from the sign of the profile congruence and, where enough loci are shared, from module_rg(), the correlation of the traits’ own allele effects.

Traits are supplied as an array in params$trait_ids. We use 2DGGI GWAS EUR ancestry meta-analysis (trait id c6d2e20f-6ece-d093-78ee-37534547c466), Triglyceride levels (trait id 1631), HDL cholesterol levels (trait id 1630), Haemoglobin A1c levels (trait id 1640).

Step 1: Univariate EBMF per trait

The existing single-trait pipeline is run independently for each target trait: run_univariate_clustering() (EBMF) followed by summarise_ebmf_programs(). Programs of high or medium confidence are kept (not low or single_trait). Each program’s posterior-mean SNP loadings (F_pm), lFSR, and high-confidence SNP membership are then extracted, with loadings sign-oriented to the target trait (see extract_program_loadings()).

Each anchor trait has its own list of duplicate traits, removed before fitting: other GWAS of the same measurement (and, for T2D, diagnosis, treatment, family-history and complication traits that repeat the T2D row at every SNP, the same list as in Suzuki T2D). A duplicate of the anchor is positive at almost every one of its SNPs after orientation, so it adds the same component to every SNP’s profile and hides the programs that actually differ between SNPs (see BMI for what that costs).

The same lists are passed to the cross-trait comparison as exclude_features: a trait listed for either anchor is left out of every profile comparison involving that anchor, including the partner’s fit, where it is an ordinary background trait. Without that, a TG duplicate sitting in the HDL fit could carry a TG ~ HDL profile match almost on its own.

Only measurements of the anchor itself are listed. Related but different measurements are kept on purpose: NMR lipoprotein-subclass traits, apolipoprotein A1, glucose traits, and haemoglobin / red-cell traits (which run opposite to HbA1c and are what separates a red-cell route to HbA1c from a glycaemic one). The table after the fit shows, for each listed trait, how closely it tracks its anchor at the anchor’s own loci.

Step 2: Common locus universe

Programs are compared at the level of the coloc_group_id (the shared fine-mapped locus) rather than the exact variant_id, because each trait carries a single candidate SNP per coloc group. Two traits can only be matched where they were fine-mapped to the same signal, which is far stricter than positional overlap, so this universe bounds how much cross-trait evidence can exist before any statistic is applied.

An uploaded GWAS numbers its coloc groups privately, so the T2D upload’s loci are first translated onto GPMap’s shared numbering through a locus bridge (the upload’s own coloc record of each partner trait, then exact variant_id, then LD). compare_program_pairs_loadings() builds those bridges, so it is run here and its scores are read in Step 3.

Step 3: Tiers – strength and direction

Every cross-trait pair of programs is asked two questions: do the same background traits drive both programs (profile congruence, phi_traits), and do the same SNPs carry them (the locus measures)? What each measure checks is explained in Multi-trait EBMF program correspondence; here they are combined.

profile_strength is strong when |φ| ≥ 0.85, moderate when 0.7 ≤ |φ| < 0.85, and weak otherwise. locus_status has three outcomes:

locus_status rule reading
too_few_loci n_loci_shared < 3 or n_eff_loci < 5 the loci cannot settle it either way
agree testable, alignment ≥ 0.5 and q_concordance ≤ 0.05 the same SNPs carry both programs
disagree testable, but alignment or q_concordance fails the shared SNPs do not carry both programs

The two are combined into a tier:

tier profile_strength locus_status reading
high strong agree same biology, carried by the same SNPs
medium strong too_few_loci same biology; too few shared loci to check the SNPs
medium moderate agree a slightly weaker profile match, confirmed by the SNPs
low strong disagree same background biology, but the shared SNPs do not carry it
low moderate too_few_loci a weaker profile match the loci cannot check
low weak agree shared loci with different biology – typical of large pleiotropic loci that every lipid or glycaemic program touches
none anything else including a moderate profile whose loci disagree

High and medium pairs are links and form the families of Step 4. The φ cutoffs follow Tucker’s conventions for factor similarity (0.85 “fair similarity”, 0.95 “equal”; Lorenzo-Seva & ten Berge, 2006), applied to genetic loadings they were not calibrated on: they are parameters of this vignette (phi_high, phi_low, min_shared_loci, min_eff_loci, min_alignment, fdr_threshold).

The direction of a link is the sign of phi_traits (direction_profile). rg (module_rg()) checks it with the two traits’ own allele effects across the loci either program claims, calling a direction only when its bootstrap interval excludes 0; it needs at least 10 usable shared loci. direction_check compares the two: agree, conflict (worth inspecting) or no call. rg never changes a tier.

column what it means
tier, profile_strength, locus_status as above
phi_traits profile congruence, signed; its sign is the direction
n_eff_traits how many background traits effectively carry the profile agreement
top_traits the background traits carrying it, with their share (negative = works against φ’s sign)
n_loci_shared, n_eff_loci shared high-confidence loci, and loci effectively carrying the locus score
alignment R² of the SNP loadings over the shared loci
comparability share of both programs’ loading on the shared loci
q_concordance BH-adjusted permutation p-value of the locus concordance
direction_profile sign of phi_traits
rg, rg_ci, direction_check allele-effect correlation, its 95% bootstrap interval, and whether its direction agrees with direction_profile (links only)

For a pair involving the uploaded T2D GWAS, rg reads both traits’ effects from the upload’s own coloc table, where the partner trait’s row in each upload coloc group sits at the upload’s lead variant and on the same allele; GPMap reports an upload’s and an existing trait’s associations on opposite alleles at about half of all variants, so the partner’s own table cannot be used. n_allele_mismatch counts loci dropped because they could not be aligned.

Step 4: Program families

Links are grouped into multi-trait families: programs are nodes, links are edges weighted by |phi_traits|, and families are the graph’s communities (Louvain modularity, build_program_families()). A program with no link belongs to no family. With mutual_best: true, a link is kept only when each of its programs is the other’s closest partner (highest |phi_traits|) in the other’s trait, so a family tends to hold one program per trait (this run: mutual_best = FALSE).

Each family is described by the loci its programs claim as high-confidence members, on GPMap’s shared locus numbering: n_core_loci are claimed by every member and n_union_loci by any. Family direction is counted from its links (n_rg_*).

Step 5: Do families recover the same biology?

Annotations play no part in forming a link, so agreement here is independent corroboration rather than a restatement of the same evidence. For each family, the high-confidence SNPs of its programs in each trait are pooled and enriched separately, against that trait’s full candidate universe, for trait categories, tissues, and Reactome and KEGG pathways (through the SNPs’ colocalising QTL genes, enrich_snp_group_pathways()). If the traits of one family independently surface the same category, tissue or pathway, the family has support beyond the loadings that created it; if they surface nothing in common, it rests on the loadings alone.

One caveat: a SNP set holding a large share of its trait’s SNPs is being compared against a background that is largely itself, so “none” for a dominant program is expected rather than informative.

Interpretation notes

  • Profile first, loci second. A link says two programs are driven by the same background traits in the same proportions (phi_traits); alignment adds whether the same SNPs carry it, where the traits share loci at all. Two traits that share few fine-mapped loci (T2D shares 36–70 with each partner here) can still share programs; their links are usually too_few_loci.
  • too_few_loci and disagree are different. The first means the loci cannot check the profile match; the second means they were checked and the shared SNPs do not carry both programs, so a strong profile match is demoted to low.
  • Read n_eff_traits and top_traits. A high congruence carried by one or two traits is a narrower claim – often “these programs both carry the same lipid fraction” – than one carried by dozens. Near-duplicate traits each count separately, so check the names, not just the number. Neither is used to gate.
  • Shared loci with different biology (weak profile, loci agree, tier low) usually means large pleiotropic loci that many programs touch, not a shared program.
  • The profile sign is a direction. Each trait’s SNPs are oriented to its own risk allele, so antagonism mirrors the profile (\(\phi < 0\)). rg, from the traits’ own allele effects, checks it where enough loci are shared; a direction_check of conflict is worth inspecting.
  • Cutoffs are judgements. 0.85 and 0.70 come from factor-analysis conventions, not from calibration on genetic loadings.
  • Sample overlap is not corrected. When the two target traits come from the same cohort, their effect estimates share participants, which biases rg away from the null. Treat rg magnitudes from same-cohort pairs as optimistic.
  • Winner’s curse. Loci enter each trait’s universe only by clearing that trait’s own significance and fine-mapping bar, so the shared set is doubly selected and effect sizes are inflated.
  • Restricted to confident programs. Only programs of high or medium confidence in their own trait are compared; Step 1 shows exactly which.
