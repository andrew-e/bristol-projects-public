Investigation of multi-trait EBMF program correspondence

This vignette extends the single-trait investigation (investigation-univariate-analysis.html) to multiple traits, and asks one question: which programs are shared between traits, and do they push the two traits the same way or opposite ways?

The default set runs this on real data with a strong, independently published prior to check against: the T2D GWAS upload from Suzuki T2D (GUID c6d2e20f-6ece-d093-78ee-37534547c466), whose high/medium programs turned out unusually clean, alongside three more specific metabolic traits rather than BMI – whose own background is dominated by dozens of body-size traits that move with it almost everywhere (see Investigation of univariate analysis) and so needs more care than a first cross-trait check calls for. Because compare_program_pairs_loadings()/compare_program_pairs_profiles() score every cross-trait pair of programs from however many traits are supplied, not just a fixed two, this is already a genuine multi-trait run: triglyceride levels (1631), HDL cholesterol (1630) and HbA1c (1640), each paired with T2D and with each other. Suzuki et al.’s own cluster profiles give concrete expectations to check the links against (Suzuki T2D, Part 5):

  • Triglycerides should link to T2D’s Lipodystrophy and Metabolic syndrome programs (P2, P6, P10, P24, P31, P35, P47 in the T2D-only fit) and be mostly concordant, but with a possible antagonistic TM6SF2-type liver locus – the one place a single partner trait is expected to show both directions at once.
  • HDL cholesterol should link to the same Lipodystrophy/Metabolic syndrome programs, antagonistically (the T2D risk allele lowers HDL).
  • HbA1c should link to T2D’s beta-cell and Residual glycaemic signal instead – a different part of the partition from the lipid traits, and the only one of the three expected to touch T2D’s glycaemic core directly.

EBMF programs are discovered independently for each target trait, so each trait’s architecture is described in its own terms first. Every cross-trait pair of programs is then scored by loading-weighted locus concordance: across the loci both traits carry, how much of the same signal do the two programs load on, and with what sign. No hard locus cutoff is applied — every shared locus contributes in proportion to how strongly both programs load on it, so the evidence grows with the amount of shared architecture rather than with how tightly a handful of loci happen to align. A permutation null calibrated per program turns that into significance, linked programs are joined into multi-trait families, and each link is then given a direction two independent ways: the sign of the concordance, and module_rg(), the correlation of the two traits’ own SNP effects across the module’s loci.

Traits are supplied as an array in params$trait_ids. We use 2DGGI GWAS EUR ancestry meta-analysis (trait id c6d2e20f-6ece-d093-78ee-37534547c466), Triglyceride levels (trait id 1631), HDL cholesterol levels (trait id 1630), Haemoglobin A1c levels (trait id 1640).

Step 1: Univariate EBMF per trait

The existing single-trait pipeline is run independently for each target trait: run_univariate_clustering() (EBMF) followed by summarise_ebmf_programs(). Programs of high or medium confidence are kept (not low or single_trait). Each program’s posterior-mean SNP loadings (F_pm), lFSR, and high-confidence SNP membership are then extracted, with loadings sign-oriented to the target trait (see extract_program_loadings()).

Each trait keeps its own proxy-trait removal before fitting. T2D’s own GWAS, diagnosis, treatment, family-history and complication traits repeat the T2D row at every SNP, exactly as in Suzuki T2D; triglycerides, HDL and HbA1c have no comparable hardcoded list here, because none of the three has BMI’s problem of dozens of traits that move with it almost everywhere (see Investigation of univariate analysis for what that costs when it is not removed). Leaving a real proxy trait in place inflates that trait’s own background similarity, which hides real programs behind one dominant, non-specific factor instead of revealing the ones that actually differ between SNPs. The T2D list below is the same one used in that vignette, reused here rather than re-derived; a trait with no list keeps every background trait.

Step 3: Loading-weighted locus concordance

Every cross-trait pair of programs is scored on the locus axis: the loci that both traits carry and that at least one of the two programs claims as a high-confidence member (n_loci_axis). Within that axis each locus contributes in proportion to how strongly it loads in both programs:

\[\text{locus\_concordance} = \sum_l w_l \, f_a(l) \, f_b(l), \qquad w_l = (1 - \text{lfsr}_a(l))(1 - \text{lfsr}_b(l))\]

Two programs that load on many of the same loci, in the same direction, score highly; a locus that matters to only one of them contributes almost nothing on its own. Loci neither program claims are left out, because every program carries a small loading at every locus and those near-zero tails can be faintly correlated between traits, which the permutation null would otherwise turn into a large concordance_z for loadings of no real size. A pair can only be linked if the two programs share at least 3 high-confidence loci (n_loci_shared, min_shared_loci). Because extract_program_loadings() orients each program so its own target trait loads positively, the sign is meaningful: positive means the two programs rise and fall together, negative means they oppose.

Step 2.5: Which axis can these traits even be compared on?

Two programs can only be matched on evidence both traits carry. There are two candidate axes, and they are very different in size.

The locus axis is the set of coloc_group_ids both traits carry. Joining on coloc group requires both traits to have been fine-mapped to the same signal, which is far stricter than positional overlap, so this axis can be small even for two well-powered traits.

The feature axis is the set of background studies both traits’ fits load on. Because every target trait is profiled against the same GPMap background universe, this axis does not require the two traits to share any loci at all.

This matters because locus_concordance is an inner product over shared loci: two programs describing the same biology at different loci score near zero by construction. If the belief is that a shared biological program need not act at identical variants, the locus axis cannot express it, and the feature axis is where the comparison has to happen.

Step 4: What counts as a link

A pair is called a link in one of two tiers.

  • primary — the two programs are each other’s best partner, and both are individually significant. Program-level significance comes from a max-statistic permutation null: it asks whether a program’s best partner is better than the best partner it would have found by chance, so it is not inflated by searching over many candidates. This is the strict, one-to-one reading and is what shared = TRUE marks.
  • candidate — the pair’s own q_concordance clears the FDR threshold but it is not a reciprocal best match. These are worth looking at because one program can genuinely correspond to two in the other trait (EBMF often splits one biological module into several factors), but they are not calibrated for the search over partners, so treat them as leads rather than conclusions.

Step 6: Direction of the shared effect (module rg)

concordance_z says whether two programs’ loading patterns line up. It does not say whether the alleles involved push the two traits the same way. module_rg() answers that directly: across the module’s loci it correlates the two traits’ own SNP effects, weighting each locus by how strongly the module loads on it and by the precision of the two estimates.

  • rg > 0 — alleles that raise trait A also raise trait B across this module.
  • rg < 0 — they oppose: the module is antagonistic between the two traits.
  • rg_ci_lower / rg_ci_upper — percentile bootstrap over loci. If the interval spans 0, the direction is not established, whatever the point estimate says.
  • n_allele_mismatch — loci dropped because the two traits’ variant_id did not agree, which would make the sign meaningless. Should be 0.

rg_direction and direction need not agree: the first is about observed allele effects, the second about latent factor structure. Where they disagree, rg with its CI is the more directly interpretable claim.

Step 7: Do linked programs recover the same biology?

Both link tiers are checked here, not just primary. A pair only needs to have cleared some significance threshold in Step 4 (mutual best match for primary, or its own q_concordance <= fdr_threshold for candidate) to be included below — the tier column in the table says which. Candidates are included deliberately: they are not corrected for the search over partners the way primary is, so this biology check is one of the best ways to tell whether a given candidate is a real lead or noise — held-out annotation agreement it wasn’t calibrated to produce is stronger support than the loading evidence alone gives it.

Annotations play no part in forming a link, so agreement here is independent corroboration rather than a restatement of the same evidence. Each program’s high-confidence SNPs are enriched separately, against its own trait’s full candidate universe, for trait categories and for tissues.

Read it as: if the two sides of a pair independently surface the same category or tissue, the link has support beyond the loading geometry that created it. If they surface nothing in common, the link rests on the loci alone.

One caveat: a program holding a large share of its trait’s SNPs is being compared against a background that is largely itself, so “none” for a dominant program is expected rather than informative.

Interpretation notes

  • Direction is anchored, not absolute. EBMF factor signs are not identifiable and are arbitrary across independent fits. extract_program_loadings() reflects both F_pm and L_pm so each program’s target-trait loading is positive, which is what makes a positive concordance_z readable as “target-aligned”.
  • Evidence scales with shared loading, not with tightness. The statistic is deliberately covariance-like. A fully normalised version (cosine_loci) cannot distinguish a pair sharing 3 loci from one sharing 30, which is why it is reported but not used to decide links.
  • Always read n_eff_loci. A large concordance_z resting on 2–3 effective loci is a thin result, and the permutation null cannot tell you that on its own.
  • Two tiers, two strengths of claim. primary is calibrated for the search over partners and is one-to-one. candidate is not, and exists because EBMF factor splitting means a single module can appear as several programs.
  • Sample overlap is not corrected. When the two target traits come from the same cohort, their effect estimates share participants, which biases module_rg away from the null. Treat rg magnitudes from same-cohort pairs as optimistic.
  • Winner’s curse. Loci enter each trait’s universe only by clearing that trait’s own significance and fine-mapping bar, so the shared set is doubly selected and effect sizes are inflated.
  • Restricted to confident programs. Only programs of high or medium confidence in their own trait are compared; Step 1 shows exactly which.
