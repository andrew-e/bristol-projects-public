Investigation of multi-trait EBMF program correspondence

This vignette extends the single-trait investigation (investigation-univariate-analysis.html) to multiple traits, and asks one question: which programs are shared between traits, and do they push the two traits the same way or opposite ways?

EBMF programs are discovered independently for each target trait, so each trait’s architecture is described in its own terms first. Every cross-trait pair of programs is then scored by loading-weighted locus concordance: across the loci both traits carry, how much of the same signal do the two programs load on, and with what sign. No hard locus cutoff is applied — every shared locus contributes in proportion to how strongly both programs load on it, so the evidence grows with the amount of shared architecture rather than with how tightly a handful of loci happen to align. A permutation null calibrated per program turns that into significance, linked programs are joined into multi-trait families, and each link is then given a direction two independent ways: the sign of the concordance, and module_rg(), the correlation of the two traits’ own SNP effects across the module’s loci.

Traits are supplied as an array in params$trait_ids. We use HDL cholesterol (trait id 1485), LDL cholesterol (trait id 1486).

Step 3: Loading-weighted locus concordance

Every cross-trait pair of programs is scored on the locus axis: the loci that both traits carry a loading for (n_loci_axis). No lFSR or magnitude threshold is applied — each locus contributes in proportion to how strongly it loads in both programs:

\[\text{locus\_concordance} = \sum_l w_l \, f_a(l) \, f_b(l), \qquad w_l = (1 - \text{lfsr}_a(l))(1 - \text{lfsr}_b(l))\]

Two programs that load on many of the same loci, in the same direction, score highly; a locus that matters to only one of them contributes almost nothing on its own. Because extract_program_loadings() orients each program so its own target trait loads positively, the sign is meaningful: positive means the two programs rise and fall together, negative means they oppose.

Scale of the comparison. n_loci_axis is how many loci each pair is scored on (the two traits’ shared coloc-group universe). n_programs_significant counts programs whose best partner beats what they would have found by chance. The two link tiers are explained in Step 4.

Step 4: What counts as a link

A pair is called a link in one of two tiers.

  • primary — the two programs are each other’s best partner, and both are individually significant. Program-level significance comes from a max-statistic permutation null: it asks whether a program’s best partner is better than the best partner it would have found by chance, so it is not inflated by searching over many candidates. This is the strict, one-to-one reading and is what shared = TRUE marks.
  • candidate — the pair’s own q_concordance clears the FDR threshold but it is not a reciprocal best match. These are worth looking at because one program can genuinely correspond to two in the other trait (EBMF often splits one biological module into several factors), but they are not calibrated for the search over partners, so treat them as leads rather than conclusions.

Step 5: Program families

Programs are nodes and primary links are edges. Because primary links are reciprocal best matches, families are one-to-one correspondences rather than transitive chains that collapse everything into one component. A program with no primary link forms its own family of one.

Step 6: Direction of the shared effect (module rg)

concordance_z says whether two programs’ loading patterns line up. It does not say whether the alleles involved push the two traits the same way. module_rg() answers that directly: across the module’s loci it correlates the two traits’ own SNP effects, weighting each locus by how strongly the module loads on it and by the precision of the two estimates.

  • rg > 0 — alleles that raise trait A also raise trait B across this module.
  • rg < 0 — they oppose: the module is antagonistic between the two traits.
  • rg_ci_lower / rg_ci_upper — percentile bootstrap over loci. If the interval spans 0, the direction is not established, whatever the point estimate says.
  • n_allele_mismatch — loci dropped because the two traits’ variant_id did not agree, which would make the sign meaningless. Should be 0.

rg_direction and direction need not agree: the first is about observed allele effects, the second about latent factor structure. Where they disagree, rg with its CI is the more directly interpretable claim.

Step 7: Do linked programs recover the same biology?

Annotations play no part in forming a link, so agreement here is independent corroboration rather than a restatement of the same evidence. Each program’s high-confidence SNPs are enriched separately, against its own trait’s full candidate universe, for trait categories and for tissues.

Read it as: if the two sides of a pair independently surface the same category or tissue, the link has support beyond the loading geometry that created it. If they surface nothing in common, the link rests on the loci alone.

One caveat: a program holding a large share of its trait’s SNPs is being compared against a background that is largely itself, so “none” for a dominant program is expected rather than informative.

Interpretation notes

  • Direction is anchored, not absolute. EBMF factor signs are not identifiable and are arbitrary across independent fits. extract_program_loadings() reflects both F_pm and L_pm so each program’s target-trait loading is positive, which is what makes a positive concordance_z readable as “target-aligned”.
  • Evidence scales with shared loading, not with tightness. The statistic is deliberately covariance-like. A fully normalised version (cosine_loci) cannot distinguish a pair sharing 3 loci from one sharing 30, which is why it is reported but not used to decide links.
  • Always read n_eff_loci. A large concordance_z resting on 2–3 effective loci is a thin result, and the permutation null cannot tell you that on its own.
  • Two tiers, two strengths of claim. primary is calibrated for the search over partners and is one-to-one. candidate is not, and exists because EBMF factor splitting means a single module can appear as several programs.
  • Sample overlap is not corrected. When the two target traits come from the same cohort, their effect estimates share participants, which biases module_rg away from the null. Treat rg magnitudes from same-cohort pairs as optimistic.
  • Winner’s curse. Loci enter each trait’s universe only by clearing that trait’s own significance and fine-mapping bar, so the shared set is doubly selected and effect sizes are inflated.
  • Restricted to valid programs. Only programs passing the per-trait validation filters are compared; Step 1 shows exactly which survived.
