Simulation study of multi-trait program correspondence

How to read this document

The question. gpmapr finds programs – groups of loci that behave alike across hundreds of background GWAS – separately for each target trait. The real-data study (investigation-multitrait-programs.Rmd) then links two traits’ programs by the loci they share, with compare_program_pairs_loadings(), and takes the direction of each link from module_rg(). This document checks that pipeline on simulated data where the answer is known. Two traits share one program, and we ask three things:

  1. Is the shared program found in each trait’s own fit, and if it is thrown away, which validation gate threw it away?
  2. Is it linked across the two traits, as the SNP overlap between the two copies falls from 1 to 0.1?
  3. Is the link given the right direction: concordant (raises both traits) or antagonistic (raises one, lowers the other)?

Programs are linked on loci only. Background-study profiles and annotations play no part in forming a link; on real data they are used afterwards, to describe programs that are already linked.

What happens in one simulated replicate.

  1. Simulate two target traits with simulate_trait_pair(). Each is a matrix of background studies x loci (z-scores). One program is planted in both traits, and K_specific programs in each trait alone.
  2. Fit each trait on its own with EBMF (run_univariate_clustering()), exactly as the real-data analysis does. EBMF knows nothing about the other trait.
  3. Validate every fitted program (summarise_ebmf_programs()), with the same gates used on real data, and record each program’s status.
  4. Link the programs across traits (compare_program_pairs_loadings()), which also runs module_rg() on every link. This is done twice: on every fitted program, and on the valid programs only, so the effect of the gates can be seen.
  5. Score discovery, links and direction against what was planted.

The whole replicate is repeated n_sim times per setting, and each table reports the mean with its Monte Carlo standard error in parentheses.

Glossary.

  • Program: one EBMF factor: a set of loci (its SNP loadings) together with the background studies that load on it.
  • SNP overlap: the fraction of the shared program’s loci that are the same locus (coloc group) in both traits. At 1 the two copies act at exactly the same loci; at 0.1 they share 4 of their 36.
  • Driver study: a background study planted as part of a program. The shared program has the same driver studies in both traits.
  • Common allele frame: effects are generated as they are reported, for one fixed allele per locus. A driver study has the same sign at a shared locus in both traits, because it is the same GWAS. An antagonistic program has opposite-sign target effects at its loci.
  • Orientation: before fitting, every locus is flipped to its own target trait’s risk-increasing allele, as on real data. This is where antagonism reaches the fit: in the antagonistic trait the driver studies end up negative.
  • status: the validation outcome of a fitted program: valid when its confidence tier is high or medium, otherwise the tier (low, or single_trait when its trait loadings rest on essentially one trait).
  • Primary / candidate link: a primary link is a mutual best match between two significant programs, the strict one-to-one claim. A candidate link is any other pair whose own FDR clears the threshold.
  • Recall: the fraction of planted shared programs linked (here 0 or 1 per replicate).
  • Precision: the fraction of reported links that are real.
  • Sign accuracy: among real links, the fraction called in the planted direction.
  • SNP precision / recall: for a resolved program, the share of its high-confidence SNPs that were planted, and the share of the planted SNPs it recovers.
  • MCSE: Monte Carlo standard error. It measures how precisely n_sim replicates pin down the reported mean, not biological variability.

Simulation design (ADEMP)

Following Morris, White and Crowther (2019, Statistics in Medicine 38:2074-2102), as the univariate studies do.

Aims. (i) Measure how often a program shared by two traits survives each trait’s own fit and validation. (ii) Measure how often it is linked across the traits as a function of SNP overlap. (iii) Measure whether the direction of the link is called correctly, by the concordance sign and by module_rg(). (iv) Measure false links when nothing is shared.

Data-generating mechanism. simulate_trait_pair() with K_shared = 1. Each trait carries n_loci_per_trait loci, of which n_shared_loci sit in a pool both traits carry. The shared program has module_size loci in each trait; a fraction snp_overlap of them are drawn from the shared pool (the rest are private to each trait), and its n_drivers_per_program driver studies are the same in both traits. Each program locus carries an effect a_s, drawn once and reused in both traits at a shared locus. A driver study’s effect at a locus is proportional to a_s; the target’s own effect is +a_s in trait A and +a_s (concordant) or -a_s (antagonistic) in trait B. Each trait also carries K_specific programs of its own, which must not be linked, and 300 unstructured background studies. The versions cross snp_overlap in {0.1, 0.2, 0.33, 0.66, 1} with the two directions, plus a null with nothing shared.

Estimand / target. For each replicate: whether the shared program is found valid in each trait, whether it is linked, and the planted direction of the link. This is a selection-plus-sign target, not a parameter.

Methods. The real-data pipeline, unchanged: run_univariate_clustering() and summarise_ebmf_programs() per trait at the shared settings, then compare_program_pairs_loadings() across traits, at its defaults: each pair is scored on the shared loci either program claims, and a pair needs at least 3 shared high-confidence loci to be linked. Linking is run twice, on every fitted program and on the valid (high- or medium-confidence) programs only; replication is computed for every program, so low-confidence programs carry the same metrics as valid ones. Direction is read two ways: the sign of concordance_z, and rg_direction from module_rg().

Performance measures. Discovered programs are mapped onto planted programs by SNP membership (map_programs_to_planted(), Jaccard at least 0.1).

  • Discovery: per trait, whether a valid program maps to the shared program; if not, the status of the best-matching program, or “not found” when no program maps to it.
  • Match quality: for the program representing each planted program, the Jaccard, SNP precision and SNP recall of its high-confidence SNPs against the planted SNPs, and the correlation of its loadings with the planted per-locus effects.
  • Linking: evaluate_multitrait_simulation() reports recall (planted shared program linked) and precision (links that are real), at the primary tier and at primary + candidate.
  • Direction: sign accuracy on true links, from the concordance sign and from module_rg(), split by planted direction. module_rg() needs at least 10 usable shared loci; when it has fewer the direction is undetermined, which is reported rather than dropped.
  • Null: under K_shared = 0 every link is a false discovery.

Monte Carlo uncertainty. Every estimate is a mean over n_sim replicates with a Monte Carlo standard error; the replicate is the MC unit. Common random numbers are used – one seed vector shared across all versions – so version-to-version differences are estimated more precisely than with independent seeds.

What this section shows. EBMF returns many more programs than were planted: the planted ones plus small factors fitted to noise. Here every fitted program is shown with its validation metrics, before any gate is applied, next to what it really is:

  • shared: its high-confidence SNPs match the shared planted program;
  • trait-specific: they match one of the trait’s own planted programs;

A gate is doing its job when it keeps the shared and trait-specific programs and removes the unmatched ones. The figure shows where each metric separates the three, which is the evidence for where a threshold should sit.

  • n_snps_filtered: SNPs passing the lFSR/magnitude membership cut; programs below min_module_size are dropped before any check.

  • replication (reported): mean Jaccard overlap between the program’s top SNPs and the best-matching factor when 20% of the background studies are held out and EBMF is refit. Computed here for every program.
  • posterior_evidence: median −log10(lFSR) of the program’s members.
  • factor_pve: share of the matrix variance the factor explains.
  • jaccard: overlap with the best-matching planted program (0 when none).

How to read it.

  • similarity_q and connectedness decide the tier. Check that shared and trait-specific programs sit above their dashed lines in the figure and unmatched ones below.
  • Replication is reported, not used for the tier. If shared and trait-specific programs cluster near 1 and unmatched ones spread lower, it separates them; where the two groups overlap, it does not.
  • The two lists are the direct evidence. Real programs removed by a gate argue for loosening it; noise passing every gate argues for tightening one.
  • Quick mode floors similarity. With quick = TRUE the similarity null uses 300 random SNP sets, so the smallest possible p is about 0.003. The full run uses 10000, which leaves more room.

2b. How closely do the resolved programs match the planted ones?

What this table shows. Section 2 counts a planted program as found when a fitted program overlaps it with Jaccard of at least 0.1, which is a low bar. This table shows how close that match actually is. For each planted program, the program that represents it is the best-matching valid program (or, if none is valid, the best-matching program of any status). Its match is then scored four ways:

  • jaccard: the overlap between its high-confidence SNPs and the planted SNPs, divided by their union.
  • snp_precision: the share of its high-confidence SNPs that were planted in the program. Below 1 means it has picked up SNPs from elsewhere.
  • snp_recall: the share of the planted SNPs it recovers as high-confidence members. Below 1 means planted SNPs were missed or went to another program.
  • loading_cor: the correlation of its SNP loadings, over every locus in the trait, with the planted per-locus effect a_s (0 outside the program). This scores the loadings themselves, not just the membership cut.

n_programs is the mean number of fitted programs mapped to the planted one. Values above 1 mean EBMF split it.

How to read it.

  • Precision and recall near 1 mean the resolved program is the planted program, locus for locus.
  • A high loading_cor means the fitted loadings also track how strongly each locus acts in the program, not only which loci are in it.
  • SNP overlap should not matter here. Each trait is fitted on its own, so the shared program’s match should look the same at every overlap. A trend with overlap would point to a problem in the simulation, not the fit.
  • Recall well below precision usually goes with n_programs above 1: the planted program was split, and the representative program holds only part of it.

How to read it. Each row block is one planted program, plus the loci that belong to no program (“unplanted”). Columns are the fitted programs that are valid or that map to a planted program. Small factors that fail validation and match nothing are left out. A clean recovery is one strong column per planted block, with dots, and pale everywhere else. Two columns sharing a block show a split program. A column lit up across several blocks shows programs merged into one. Dots outside a block’s own column are high-confidence SNPs assigned to the wrong program.

How to read it. The concordance sign is expected to read about 1 for concordant and about 0 for antagonistic programs. That is not noise: each trait’s loci are oriented to its own risk allele before fitting, so a shared locus loads positively in both traits’ programs whatever the direction, and the sign of concordance_z cannot express antagonism. module_rg() correlates the two targets’ own effects in a common allele frame, so it should read close to 1 in both directions, with mean_rg positive for concordant and negative for antagonistic. Notes for interpreting runs

  • One shared program, isolated. Each planted program has its own driver studies and its own loci, so the fits here are easy. This checks that the pipeline recovers and links a shared program when nothing else interferes; it does not test programs that share driver studies or loci with other programs in the same trait.
  • Recall is credited per planted program, not per link. EBMF can split one planted program into several factors, so counting links would reward splitting.
  • Sign accuracy is conditional on a real link. A high value alongside a low recall means the few links found were directionally right, not that direction is well recovered overall.
  • Driver overlap is fixed at 1. At a shared locus the same background GWAS are present in both traits’ matrices, so the shared program’s driver studies are identical in both. Only SNP overlap varies.
  • Background realism is off by default. background_corr and snp_pleiotropy_sd default to 0, the historical independent, random-signed background. The discovery and linking results are largely insensitive to that, but the null is not: run the realism arm of the grid script before quoting a false-link rate.
  • Sample overlap is not simulated. Two real target traits from the same cohort share participants, which biases cross-trait statistics, and module_rg() in particular, away from the null.
  • Results are not comparable with earlier renders. Earlier versions of this document planted antagonism by flipping driver signs and gave the target a flat effect at every locus, and compared a background-study profile axis alongside the locus axis. Effects are now generated in a common allele frame, and only the locus axis is used.
